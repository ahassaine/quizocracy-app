What is a day's plan and how long does it take?#

Each child has a short list of activities for the day, each with a target of right answers (for example: maths 8, times tables 20, spelling 8). Wrong answers never count towards a target. The day is finished when every activity reaches its target, and the bar shows how far along it is. A child can keep going after that; extra right answers show as “+5”, and in grammar and science the extra questions take the year's topics in turn, the weakest first.

When you add a child, the plan is filled in for their school year and comes to about 15 minutes. The child's Settings page shows “About … minutes a day” as you change the targets. Younger children take longer per answer, and the estimate allows for that.

How does something get learned (mastered)?#

  • Maths, grammar and science skills have a score from 0 to 100. Right answers raise it; wrong answers lower it, more so near the top. At 100 the skill is secure (mastered). A child who gets a skill right every time, 5 in a row, is secure at once, and the app checks it again on a later day. Secure skills come back now and then so they are not forgotten. From Year 5, a new maths skill starts with questions at the middle of the year's level, and easier ones come only after a wrong answer.
  • Times tables: a fact is known once it is answered right 3 times in a row.
  • Spelling: a word is learned when it is spelt right on 3 different days. Words come back after 1, 3, 7 and 21 days.
  • Irregular verbs: a verb is known when it is right twice running; 10 right in a row moves the child up a level.

This takes days and weeks, not one evening. A right answer typed just after being shown the answer counts for today's target, but not towards learning it.

Why does a missed question come back later?#

After a wrong answer the child sees the right one. If the same question came straight back, they could copy it from memory without learning it. So it comes back after at least 5 other questions and 3 minutes, when getting it right means it was remembered. Grammar and science questions come back once more on another day. In grammar and science, a one-word answer with only a spelling slip, or a rewritten sentence missing only its full stop or capital letter, gets one “Nearly” and a second try first.

On their own page a child also has a Practise my mistakes button. On the child's progress page you see “Mistakes to go over” for the last 7 days. A mistake moves to “Put right since” only when the child later answers that same question right: the same word, times-table fact, verb or grammar and science question. Maths questions are made fresh each time, so there a mistake moves when a question like it (the same kind of question, with new numbers) is answered right later; the page then says “a question like this was right later”. A right answer to some other question on the same skill doesn't count, and neither does a right answer typed straight after being shown the answer. Once put right, a mistake stays put right; missing it again shows as a new mistake. Practise my mistakes asks those same questions again (in maths, a question like each one).

What do “right first time”, “rushed” and “copied” mean?#

  • Right first time: the share of first tries that were right. A question answered again after a mistake is not a first try.
  • Rushed: wrong answers given in under 1.5 seconds, left blank, or typed as random keys (like “asdf”). A few are normal; many suggest the child is racing to finish.
  • Copied?: right answers typed within a minute of being shown that answer after a mistake. They count for the day's target, but we flag them so you can see how much was remembered rather than copied.

PINs: setting, showing, a locked child and unlocking#

  • Setting: you choose a 4-number PIN when you add a child. To change it, a parent opens the child's Settings and types a new PIN. The new one works at once and the old one stops working.
  • Showing: forgotten it? A parent presses Show the PIN in the child's Settings. A carer with their own login can see the PINs too: in Settings (the carer's own Settings page), under Children's PINs, press Show next to the child.
  • Locked: after 6 wrong PINs within 5 minutes, that child's tile locks for 5 minutes, so a brother or sister can't keep guessing. The tile says until when.
  • Unlocking: wait, or on Home press Unlock next to the child. On “Who's learning?” open Grown-up? Unlock … now under the child's name and type your password (any grown-up on this family can use their own). Changing the PIN also lifts the lock.

Changing targets: “Changes start tomorrow”#

Change the activities and targets in the child's Settings. If the child hasn't practised yet today, changes start straight away. If they have started, today keeps the plan they started with and the changes start tomorrow, so the finish line doesn't move under them. To change today too, tick Change today's plan too; a reward already earned today stays earned. Past days are always judged against the plan they had.

The school's spelling list#

Got this week's spelling list from school? Open the child's Settings and find This week's spelling list from school (Home has a link to it under each child). Type or paste the words: commas, spaces or one word a line all work, and a numbered list copied from a school letter is fine. Press Save.

For the next 7 days the child practises those words first, with the same listening and marking, then goes back to their year's list. Saving a new list starts a new week; emptying the box stops it. Words from earlier lists that are still tricky keep coming back until they are learned, and the progress page shows how each word is going.

Words that sound alike (there, their and they're; where and wear; went and want): add a short sentence after a dash so the child knows which one is meant, for example their - it is their dog. We hide the word in the sentence (“it is ___ dog”) and show it with the question. Each word with a sentence goes on its own line, or between commas: their - it is their dog, should, could. If a word in the list sounds like another and has no sentence, the form asks you about it.

Year 4 times tables check practice#

In June of Year 4, children in England take the Multiplication Tables Check: 25 times-table questions, 6 seconds each, on a screen. The practice works the same way. A child in Year 3 or above finds it at the bottom of the Times tables page: Practise the check. They see their score at the end and the questions they missed; Practise the ones I missed then practises only that check's missed facts. From the second miss of the same fact, a tip for remembering it appears.

Every check, with its date and the best score, is on the child's progress page and in the printable report. To make one check a day part of the plan, tick + a Year 4 check next to Times tables in the child's Settings (Years 3 to 6). A finished check also counts towards the times tables target when times tables are in the plan.

Days off, rest days, sick days and holidays#

  • Practice days: in the child's Settings, under Practice days, untick the days they don't practise (for example Saturday and Sunday). Those become rest days every week.
  • A day off (a sick day, a holiday, a busy weekend): in the child's Settings, under Days off, choose the first day and, if it is more than one, the last day (up to 3 weeks), then press Save. A day off starts at once, even today. To cancel one, tick it in the list there and save. Home has a Day off? Mark it link under each child.

On a rest day nothing is due: the child's page says so, anything they do counts as extra, and the history shows the day striped as rest, not as 0%. A reward already earned is never taken back. A carer can mark a day off too.

Reading settings and dyslexia#

Each child can have the questions shown in a way that is easier for them to read:

  • Bigger text
  • Easy-to-read letters, a font that is good for dyslexia
  • More space between letters and lines
  • Less movement (no stars or bouncing) and stronger colours

A parent sets them in the child's Settings, under Reading. They belong to that child, so they follow them to every phone, tablet and computer, and a brother or sister on the same tablet keeps their own. The child can change them too, with the Aa button at the top of their page and of every question. With no child signed in, the Aa button changes that device only.

Every question can also be read out: see Read-aloud.

Rewards#

Set a family reward in Settings (for example “30 minutes of screen time”) and when it is earned: at 100% of the day, or at a lower share such as 80%. A child can have their own reward instead, in their own Settings. Children see the reward on their page and on the family board, and Home shows when it is earned. Once earned, it stays earned for that day, whatever changes later. The app only tells you; giving the reward is up to you.

Handing the device to a child, and parent mode#

Press Hand over to a child on Home (or Learn at the top). This locks the parent area and opens “Who's learning?”. Your child taps their name, types their PIN and presses Start.

To come back, press Parent at the top and type your password (any parent or carer of the family can use their own). On a device a child has used, parent mode locks itself after 5 minutes without use. A child left signed in goes back to “Who's learning?” after an hour without use. After 8 wrong parent passwords the parent area stays locked for 15 minutes.

Siblings on one device#

Each child has their own name tile, PIN, year, plan, reading settings and progress. When one finishes, they press Switch: that signs them out, and the next child types their own PIN. Nobody can practise on a sibling's account without the sibling's PIN. The Board shows everyone's day side by side.

A child using their own phone or tablet#

Children don't have a login of their own: they use the family's, with their PIN. To set up a child's own phone or tablet, open quizocracy.com on it, sign in once with your (a grown-up's) email and password, and press Hand over to a child. From then on it opens on “Who's learning?”, and the child signs in with their PIN. You can also put it on the home screen, like an app (see Putting Quizocracy on your home screen).

Your password is not kept on the phone in a way the child can see or use: going back to the parent area always asks for a grown-up's password, and it locks itself after 5 minutes without use. When you sign in on a new device, we email you to say so. If you change your password, every device is signed out, the child's phone too, and a grown-up signs in on it again.

A second parent or a carer#

Each grown-up has their own email and password on the same family. In Settings, under Grown-ups on this family, type their email, choose Parent or Carer and press Send invitation. They get an email with a link that works for 7 days and choose their own password. The parents are told by email when someone joins.

  • Parent (for a partner): can do everything, like you. Add and change children and their plans, invite and remove grown-ups, change the account. Deleting a child needs that parent's password, and the other parents get an email about it.
  • Carer (for a grandparent or a childminder): can hand the device to a child, unlock a locked child, see Home, the board and each child's progress, see the children's PINs in their own Settings, type in the school's spelling list and mark a day off. A carer can't change the children's plans, targets or rewards, delete a child, or change the account or its data.

Changing a role: next to their name in Settings, press Make carer or Make parent. Their login, password and weekly email stay as they were, and we tell them by email. Removing someone: press Remove next to their name. They are signed out on every device at once and get an email saying so. The account owner (the grown-up who signed the family up) can't be removed or changed. Only parents can invite, change or remove grown-ups.

Read-aloud#

Every question has a speaker button that reads it out with the device's own voice (a British English voice where the device has one). Children in Years 1 and 2 also hear each question as it appears, and hear the feedback. On iPhone and iPad the device only speaks after a first tap, so the button says “Tap to hear” until then. Spelling always plays the word to spell. If a device has no voice, the speaker button is not shown and everything else works as normal. For bigger text or an easier font, see Reading settings.

Which curriculum does it follow?#

Follows the national curriculum in England (Years 1–9). Maths, grammar and punctuation, and science follow each year's programme of study, skill by skill. Spelling uses the national curriculum word lists for Years 1 to 6, and longer, harder words for Years 7 to 9. Times tables start in Year 2 and irregular verbs in Year 3.

In Scotland, Wales or Northern Ireland? Pick the year in England that matches your child's age. For example, P4 in Scotland is about Year 3. Birthday cut-offs differ a little between the nations, so if your child falls between two years, start with the lower one: you can change the year at any time in the child's Settings.

SATs and other tests (KS1, KS2)#

  • Year 6 (KS2 SATs, in May): the tests are maths (arithmetic and reasoning), reading, and grammar, punctuation and spelling. Year 6 maths here follows the Year 6 programme, which goes back over the earlier KS2 topics the maths papers also ask about: place value, written methods, times tables, fractions and decimals. Secure skills keep coming back now and then, so earlier work is not forgotten. In Year 6 grammar, the Test review topic goes over the Year 3 to 5 grammar that the KS2 grammar, punctuation and spelling test asks about (word classes, clauses, tenses, apostrophes); the other Year 6 topics are the Year 6 grammar itself. Spelling uses the Years 5 and 6 word list, and the spelling test draws on it too. Reading comprehension is not covered.
  • Year 4 (the Multiplication Tables Check, in June): see Year 4 times tables check practice.
  • Year 2 (KS1): the KS1 tests are now optional for schools. Year 2 maths, grammar and spelling here follow the Year 2 programme, which is what those tests are about.

Quizocracy is daily practice of the curriculum, not past papers or timed mock tests (apart from the Year 4 check practice). If the class is revising one topic, choose it under Working on this at school in the child's Settings.

“Something wrong with this question?”#

Under every question there is a Something wrong with this question? link. A child (or you) can say the answer looks wrong, the question can't be read, or it doesn't make sense, and add a note. We look at every report.

You see each child's reports, and what we found, on the child's progress page, under Questions … reported, and the mistake it was about is marked “reported”. To get an email when we have checked one, tick Email me when a question a child reported has been checked in Settings. If a child reports a right answer as wrong (“I think 8 × 7 is 54”), it can be a good one to go over together.

A progress report for a teacher#

On a child's progress page, press Print / save as PDF. That opens a progress report: days practised, answers, right first time, minutes, skills secure by subject and strand, what to work on next and the spellings learned, for the last 7 days, the last 30 days or this term so far. Press Print / save as PDF again there to print it or save it to share.

The weekly email#

On Sunday evening each grown-up who has it on gets one email about the week (Monday to Sunday): for each child, the days done, answers and minutes, what they mastered, and up to three things to help with. A week with no practice at all sends nothing.

It is on for parents and off for carers to start with. Each grown-up turns their own on or off in Settings, under Email me a weekly summary, or with the one-click unsubscribe link at the bottom of the email.

Putting Quizocracy on your home screen#

  • iPhone or iPad (Safari): press Share (the square with an arrow), then Add to Home Screen.
  • Android (Chrome): open the ⋮ menu, then Add to Home screen or Install app.
  • Computer (Chrome or Edge): press the install icon at the right of the address bar.

It then opens full screen on “Who's learning?”, like an app. It still needs the internet to check answers.

What does the beta include?#

Everything, free: all seven activities (maths, times tables, spelling, grammar and punctuation, science, numbers in words and irregular verbs), rewards, the family board and room for up to 6 children. Nothing is charged and no card is asked for. We will tell you before anything you rely on changes. Tell us what works and what doesn't: it shapes what we build next.

Data and privacy#

We keep your email address, your children's first names or nicknames, their school years and PINs, and their answers, so we can choose the next question and show you progress. No surnames, dates of birth, photos or schools. No adverts, no chat, nothing shared with other families, and no AI looks at your child's work. In Settings you can download everything we hold, or delete your account and all its data at once. Read the full privacy notice.

How to contact us#

Email {{ cfg.SUPPORT_EMAIL }}. A real person reads every message. If something looks wrong, tell us the child's year, the activity and the question, and a screenshot helps.
